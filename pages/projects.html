Research projects

My research combines computational genomics, multi-omics, and machine learning to understand disease mechanisms and move from genetic association toward actionable regulatory biology.

01 · CURRENT

Brain disorders and therapeutic target discovery

Multi-omicsBrain tissueGene regulationCommon & rare disorders

Question: How do genetic risk and regulatory state interact across developmental time to produce brain disorders, and which molecular mechanisms are most amenable to therapeutic intervention?

Approach: Integrate transcriptomic, epigenomic, chromatin-accessibility, and DNA-sequencing data from human brain tissue across developmental stages and cell types. Use computational genomics and machine learning to map disease-associated regulatory programs and prioritise candidate cis-regulatory elements.

Translational direction: Connect mechanistic inference to functional perturbation, including CRISPRa/i, to test whether regulatory elements can be leveraged as therapeutic entry points and ultimately support precision medicine strategies for brain disorders.

02 · EARLIER

Computational drug & disease prediction

Machine learningBioinformaticsDrug interactionsCancer subtyping

Focus: Earlier research centred on developing computational models for biomedical prediction, with a particular emphasis on extracting clinically relevant signal from high-dimensional molecular and drug-related data.

Examples: Neural-network and matrix-factorisation approaches for drug–drug interaction prediction, semi-supervised approaches for drug–disease association, and deep-clustering methods for breast-cancer molecular subtyping.

Why it matters: These projects established the computational and bioinformatics foundation that now supports my work in multi-omic brain disease research.

PROJECT 03 · COMPLETED

Machine learning for drug–drug interaction & disease-association prediction

Neural networksMatrix factorizationBioinformatics

Question: Can integrated similarity measures between drugs — chemical, target, and side-effect profiles — predict interactions and disease associations that haven't yet been observed clinically?

Approach: Built neural-network (NDD) and integrated similarity-constrained matrix factorization (ISCMF) models, plus a semi-supervised graph-cut method for drug–disease association prediction.

Status: Completed; published in Scientific Reports and Network Modeling Analysis in Health Informatics and Bioinformatics.
